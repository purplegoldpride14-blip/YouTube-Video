"Right Here With You" is a late-1980s R&B ballad from Marcus Vale about the promise that's easiest to make and the hardest one to keep — and what's left in the room after you've broken it anyway.

The video opens and closes on the same shot: Marcus alone in a dim apartment chair, late at night, something of hers still draped over the chair beside him. Everything in between is how the room got this quiet.

**The Good Times**
Before any of it went wrong, there was a kitchen full of morning light, a record on the turntable, slow dancing in the living room with nowhere else either of them needed to be. These scenes aren't filler — they're the whole reason the rest of the video hurts. A woman who isn't in any of Marcus's earlier videos steps into this one: same era, same warmth, a different person entirely, and for a while, everything between them is easy.

**The Cracks Begin**
The apartment doesn't change, but the light in it does. Conversations get shorter. The couch gets wider, somehow, with the same two people sitting on it. Nobody says what's actually wrong, which is exactly the problem.

**A Last Good Night**
Before it ends, there's one more night out — a booth in a late-night diner under a buzzing neon sign, then up on the apartment rooftop with the city skyline behind them. It's the kind of night that would have been perfect if it hadn't also been the last one. A hand almost reaches for a hand and doesn't quite make it.

**The Argument**
Back in the apartment, the hushed argument everyone's been avoiding finally happens, and the suitcase comes down from the closet. Two people standing on opposite sides of the same room, with an open suitcase between them doing the talking neither of them will.

**The Goodbye**
One long look at the door. No shouting, no last-minute reversal — just a suitcase picked up, a hallway, and a door easing shut on its own.

**Alone, Again**
The video ends exactly where it started: Marcus in the same chair, the same lamp, the same scarf left behind. Nothing in the room has changed except that the one thing that made it feel full isn't there anymore.

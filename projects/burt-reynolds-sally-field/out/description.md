"She Was the One Who Got Away": Burt Reynolds and Sally Field

The Meeting

In 1976, Burt Reynolds and Sally Field were cast opposite each other in Smokey and the Bandit, a car chase comedy nobody expected to become one of the biggest hits of the year, second at the box office only to Star Wars. Reynolds was already one of the country's biggest movie stars. Field was best known for television and was eager to be taken seriously as an actress. The two had known each other only a handful of days before their chemistry became obvious to everyone on set. Reynolds later said it felt less like four days and more like four years. Off screen, they quickly became one of Hollywood's most-watched couples.

The Golden Years

Over the next five years, Reynolds and Field made three more films together: Hooper, The End, and Smokey and the Bandit Two. Reynolds proposed marriage more than once during this period, and Field turned him down every time, later saying she always sensed his heart wasn't fully behind the offer. It was an early sign that the two of them were experiencing the relationship very differently, even while sharing a home and a career.

The Turning Point: Norma Rae

In 1979, Field was offered the lead role in Norma Rae, a drama about a textile worker who organizes her factory into a labor union. According to Field, Reynolds objected to the role, made a demeaning comment about the character, and threw the script at her rather than simply saying no. Field took the part anyway, and by her account, Reynolds then tried to discourage her from attending the film's premiere at the Cannes Film Festival. She went regardless. Norma Rae won her the first of her two Academy Awards for Best Actress. Field later said this experience marked the beginning of her pulling away from the relationship for good. The two split for the final time in 1982.

Two Different Stories

What makes this story unusual isn't the breakup itself, but what happened over the following three decades. Reynolds never stopped describing Field as the love of his life, calling her "the one who got away" in interview after interview, well into his seventies. In his 2015 memoir, But Enough About Me, he devoted an entire chapter to their relationship and called ending it one of his biggest regrets. Field's own account, told publicly and later in her 2018 memoir In Pieces, was far more complicated. She described the relationship as often controlling and emotionally difficult, connected it to her own childhood experience of abuse by her stepfather, the actor and stuntman Jock Mahoney, and revealed that she had not spoken to Reynolds for roughly the last thirty years of his life. Reynolds died in 2018, the same year In Pieces was published. Field said she was glad he would never read what she had written about their years together, because it would have hurt him.
